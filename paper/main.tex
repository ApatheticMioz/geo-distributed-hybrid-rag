%  ABSTRACT
% ===========================================================================
\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems are increasingly deployed across
geographically distributed, heterogeneous nodes, yet the placement of their
individual pipeline stages---sparse retrieval, dense retrieval, fusion,
hydration, and generation---remains an open systems question. We present a
cache-state-aware measurement study of stage-to-node placement for a
\emph{hybrid} RAG pipeline (BM25 + BGE-M3 dense retrieval with
reciprocal-rank fusion, and a vLLM-served Qwen3.8-27B generator) on commodity
hardware: an RTX~3090 generation host and a GTX~1660~Ti laptop gateway,
connected over a WireGuard link shaped by \emph{bidirectional} Linux
\texttt{netem} across four WAN regimes (0/15/40/80~ms), with 50 seeded queries
per arm, three repeats, and cold-prefix and warm cache states reported
separately. Three findings reframe placement for this class of systems.
First, TTFT is essentially \emph{flat} across the swept RTT range (a measured
sensitivity of 0.73~ms of TTFT per ms of RTT): an identifier-sized placement
boundary makes the network regime a second-order concern. Second, KV-cache
state, not the network, dominates latency variance---cold-prefix TTFT
($\approx$3.5~s) is $4.4\times$ warm TTFT ($\approx$0.82~s). Third,
\textbf{SPHP} (Speculative Progressive Hydration \& Prefill), which prefills
the generator on a provisional sparse hint while the dense leg is still in
flight, cuts cold TTFT by 17.4\% (95\%~CI $[-20.1,-14.3]\%$) at every regime
with a measured hit rate of $h=0.84$, while warm-state savings are within
noise; an answer-level comparison bounds the hit-path quality cost (token-F1
0.42 versus a 0.59 resampling-noise ceiling). We further derive an analytical
placement cost model validated at the regime level (leave-one-tier-out MAPE
1.3--2.8\%) and use it to show that the SPHP-augmented split placement is
optimal across the entire swept (RTT, bandwidth) space. All artifacts,
including the measurement harness and scoring pipeline, are released.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation; hybrid search; edge--cloud systems;
speculative inference; geo-distributed inference; placement optimization;
KV-cache-aware measurement.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

%  I. INTRODUCTION

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has become the dominant architecture for
grounding large language models (LLMs) in external knowledge
\cite{lewis2020rag,guu2020realm,borgeaud2022retro,gao2024ragsurvey}. A modern
RAG pipeline is not a single monolith but a sequence of heterogeneous stages:
a \emph{sparse} lexical search (e.g., BM25 \cite{robertson1994bm25}), a
\emph{dense} semantic search over learned embeddings
\cite{karpukhin2020dpr,reimers2019sbert,chen2024bge}, a \emph{fusion} step that
merges the two ranked lists (e.g., reciprocal-rank fusion
\cite{cormack2009rrf}), a \emph{hydration} step that fetches the full text of
the selected passages, and finally \emph{generation}, in which an LLM
\cite{vaswani2017transformer,qwen3technicalreport} is prefilled with the
retrieved context and autoregressively decodes an answer
\cite{kwon2023vllm}.

As these systems move from a single datacenter box to the edge, a fundamental
\emph{placement dilemma} emerges. The stages have wildly different resource
profiles: dense retrieval and generation demand a GPU, sparse retrieval is
CPU-friendly, and hydration is a cheap disk lookup. Large models require
hardware most machines do not have, data-residency rules can force retrieval
and generation onto different hosts, and cost pushes work toward whatever
hardware exists. Yet, to the best of our knowledge, the literature cannot
answer: \emph{where should each stage of a hybrid RAG pipeline run, under which
network conditions, and what exactly is lost when the topology degrades?}
DNN partitioning is studied at the layer level
\cite{kang2017neurosurgeon,hung2020cloudedge,zhang2021cloudedge} and LLM
serving splits model \emph{phases} across machines, but a measurement study of
\emph{which pipeline stage runs on which machine} in a multi-node hybrid RAG
deployment is missing.

We close this gap with a deployed, instrumented two-node testbed and a
cache-state-aware factorial campaign over placement variant $\times$ network
regime. Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1 (Measurement).} A cache-state-aware factorial measurement of
hybrid RAG placement on commodity heterogeneous hardware (RTX~3090 + GTX~1660~Ti)
over a WireGuard link shaped by \emph{bidirectional} \texttt{netem}
\cite{donenfeld2017wireguard,hemminger2000netem} across four WAN regimes, with
seeded disjoint query slices, warmup discard, three repeats, and percentile +
bootstrap-CI reporting. We show that (i) TTFT is flat across 0--80~ms of RTT
(slope 0.73~ms/ms), because the P2 boundary ships only identifier-sized
payloads, and (ii) KV-cache state dominates TTFT variance: cold-prefix TTFT is
$4.4\times$ warm TTFT (\S\ref{sec:characterization}).

\item \textbf{C2 (Mechanism).} \textbf{SPHP}, a pipeline-level speculative
protocol, measured for real: a provisional sparse hint is shipped to the
generation node, a masked speculative prefill runs in parallel with the dense
leg, and the result is reconciled against the fused context on arrival
(set-overlap hit test). With a \emph{measured} hit rate of $h=0.84$, SPHP
reduces cold-prefix TTFT by 17.4\% at every regime; warm-state savings are
within noise; and an answer-level comparison bounds the hit-path quality cost
(\S\ref{sec:sphp}).

\item \textbf{C3 (Model and policy).} An analytical placement cost model whose
macro predictions are validated at the decision granularity that matters---the
network regime (leave-one-tier-out MAPE 1.3--2.8\% for P2)---plus a crossover
analysis showing that P2-SPHP is the optimal placement across the entire swept
(RTT, bandwidth) space, and a live demonstration of the regime-level policy
selecting placement (\S\ref{sec:costmodel}--\S\ref{sec:evaluation}).

\item \textbf{C4 (Artifacts).} A fully open, reproducible testbed: the
8.84M-passage MS~MARCO index \cite{bajaj2018msmarco}, the benchmark harness,
the scoring pipeline, the cost model, and all figures---every number in this
paper regenerates from committed scripts and seeded run files
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
\end{itemize}

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
\S\ref{sec:related} reviews related work on RAG, hybrid retrieval,
speculative inference, and edge--cloud systems. \S\ref{sec:architecture}
describes the two-node deployment and the placement taxonomy.
\S\ref{sec:characterization} presents the empirical characterization.
\S\ref{sec:sphp} details the SPHP
protocol. \S\ref{sec:costmodel} develops the analytical placement cost model
and the crossover analysis. \S\ref{sec:evaluation} reports the evaluation, and
\S\ref{sec:discussion}--\S\ref{sec:conclusion} discuss the results and
conclude.

%  II. RELATED WORK
% ===========================================================================
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
RAG couples a parametric generator with a non-parametric memory. Lewis
\emph{et al.}~\cite{lewis2020rag} introduced the generator--retriever
formulation for knowledge-intensive tasks; REALM~\cite{guu2020realm} and
RETRO~\cite{borgeaud2022retro} showed that retrieving from trillions of tokens
improves language modeling at scale. A recent survey
\cite{gao2024ragsurvey} organizes the field into naive, advanced, and
modular RAG, and active-retrieval variants such as FLARE
\cite{jiang2023flare} and the declarative compiler DSPy \cite{khattab2023dspy}
adapt retrieval to the query. All of this work assumes a single, well-provisioned
host; none addresses the placement of the retrieval and generation stages
across a geo-distributed, heterogeneous fleet.

\subsection{Hybrid Retrieval and Fusion}
Hybrid search combines a lexical (sparse) and a semantic (dense) signal. BM25
\cite{robertson1994bm25} remains the lexical workhorse; dense passage retrieval
\cite{karpukhin2020dpr} and sentence embeddings \cite{reimers2019sbert} provide
the semantic leg, with multi-vector late-interaction models such as ColBERT
\cite{khattab2020colbert} improving effectiveness at higher cost. Modern
multi-functionality embedding models such as BGE-M3 \cite{chen2024bge} serve
both dense and lexical objectives from one encoder. Reciprocal-rank fusion
\cite{cormack2009rrf} is the standard parameter-light combiner. We use exactly
this stack---Tantivy BM25, BGE-M3 dense search over Qdrant, and RRF---and
measure how its two legs behave when split across machines.

\subsection{Speculative LLM Inference}
Speculative decoding accelerates autoregressive generation by drafting with a
cheap model and verifying with a large one
\cite{leviathan2023speculative,chen2023speculative}. Our SPHP mechanism is
\emph{orthogonal} to this line: rather than speculating over the
\emph{tokens} of a single model, we speculate over the \emph{pipeline
schedule}---we begin prefilling the generation node on a provisional (sparse)
context before the dense leg has finished, then reconcile. This is a form of
pipeline-level speculation that exploits the measured structure of the
retrieval legs (\S\ref{sec:characterization}).

\subsection{Edge--Cloud and Geo-Distributed Systems}
Hybrid cloud--edge inference partitions a network between a device and a
server \cite{kang2017neurosurgeon,hung2020cloudedge,zhang2021cloudedge}, and
datacenter-scale serving systems study phase and memory management
\cite{kwon2023vllm,zaharia2016datacenter}. Secure point-to-point tunnels such
as WireGuard \cite{donenfeld2017wireguard} and kernel traffic control
(\texttt{tc}/\texttt{netem}) \cite{hemminger2000netem} provide the transport
and the controlled-WAN substrate we use. Energy and policy considerations for
distributed deep learning \cite{strubell2019energy} motivate placing expensive
stages where hardware and power are cheapest. Our work is the first to bring
this placement lens to the RAG pipeline itself.

\section{System Architecture \& Deployment}
\label{sec:architecture}

\subsection{Testbed}
We deploy a two-node hybrid RAG system over a WireGuard virtual private
network. \textbf{Node~A} (generation) is an RTX~3090 workstation
(24~GB VRAM) serving a Qwen3.8-27B model quantized to W4A16
\cite{qwen3technicalreport} under vLLM \cite{kwon2023vllm}, together with a
local SQLite store holding the full 8{,}841{,}823-passage MS~MARCO corpus
\cite{bajaj2018msmarco} for $O(1)$ hydration. The engine runs with
speculative decoding enabled; because the model emits chain-of-thought
deliberation by default, thinking is \emph{suppressed per request} via
chat-template kwargs so that TTFT measures the answer stream rather than
reasoning tokens. \textbf{Node~B} (edge gateway)
is a laptop (Intel i7-10750H, GTX~1660~Ti) running the retrieval stack: a
Tantivy BM25 index over the full 8.84M passages, a BGE-M3 dense encoder
\cite{chen2024bge} with a 1024-dimensional HNSW index in Qdrant, and local
reciprocal-rank fusion \cite{cormack2009rrf}. The two nodes are connected over
WireGuard \cite{donenfeld2017wireguard} (Node~A at 10.8.0.1, Node~B at
10.8.0.2) with gRPC streaming for the query, the fused context, and the
returned token stream. Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the architecture and the
warm steady-state per-stage timing annotations.

\subsection{Placement Topologies}
We define a taxonomy of five placement topologies by the location of the
pipeline boundary (Figure~\ref{fig:topologies}):
\textbf{P0} (colocated) runs every stage on Node~A; \textbf{P1} places only the
gateway on Node~B with all compute on Node~A; \textbf{P2} (our baseline) runs
sparse, dense, and fusion on Node~B and hydration, prefill, and decode on
Node~A, shipping only the top-$k$ document identifiers across the link;
\textbf{P2-SPHP} augments P2 with the speculative protocol of
\S\ref{sec:sphp}; and \textbf{P3} moves hydration to Node~B as well, shipping
the full text of the top passages. P2 is the natural split: it keeps the
GPU-bound dense retrieval and generation on capable hardware while moving the
cheap identifier payload---not the bulky corpus text---across the link.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/fig2_topologies.pdf}
\caption{Placement topology taxonomy (P0--P3). Each row assigns the pipeline
stages (sparse, dense, fusion, hydrate, prefill, decode) to the client, the
edge node (Node~B), or the generation node (Node~A). P2-SPHP is P2 with a
speculative prefill that overlaps the dense leg.}
\label{fig:topologies}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{Data Flow and Protocol}
The client submits a query to the Node~B gateway over HTTP; the gateway
dispatches it to both retrieval legs concurrently over gRPC and, on completion,
streams the fused top-$k$ context (or the provisional sparse hint under SPHP)
to Node~A over the WireGuard tunnel. Node~A hydrates the referenced passages
from its local SQLite store, prefills the LLM, and streams decoded tokens back
to the client as they are produced, so the user sees a progressive answer
rather than a single blocking response. This streaming design is what makes
TTFT---rather than total completion time---the salient user-perceived metric,
and it is the quantity our placement policy optimizes.

\subsection{Network Control}
To study placement under controlled WAN conditions we shape the link with
Linux \texttt{netem} \cite{hemminger2000netem} \emph{bidirectionally}: a
delaying qdisc on Node~A's egress interface applies $\mathrm{rtt}/2$ of
propagation to outbound packets, and an ingress redirect
(\texttt{ifb}) applies $\mathrm{rtt}/2$ to inbound packets, so that an
end-to-end ping RTT increases by exactly the configured value. We validate
the shaping before every measured window with a ping probe against a
$\pm$30\% gate (e.g., the 15~ms regime measured 14.3~ms). We sweep four
regimes---0, 15, 40, and 80~ms RTT---representing LAN, near-edge, and
wide-area conditions, over the nominal 100~Mbps WireGuard link. Because both
directions are shaped, every crossing of the A--B boundary (query forward,
context return, hint forward, token stream back) experiences the configured
delay---unlike an application-level sleep injected at one endpoint, which
inflates only the metric it touches and reproduces no queuing or
serialization behavior.

\subsection{Measurement Protocol}
All reported numbers come from a single scripted campaign: four regimes
$\times$ two arms (P2 baseline, P2-SPHP) $\times$ 50 queries sampled with
seed~42 from the MS~MARCO dev set (6{,}980 queries with qrels), with
\emph{disjoint per-arm query slices} so that no query is seen by both arms and
cross-arm cache warm-up cannot contaminate comparisons. Each arm executes five
warm-up queries that are discarded, then three recorded repeats per query
(1{,}200 measured records, zero failures). Repeat~0 of each arm slice is a
\emph{cold-prefix} reference (the engine retains no flush endpoint, so we
report this state explicitly rather than pretending order-independence);
repeats~1--2 are \emph{warm}. We report means, medians, p95, and bootstrap
95\% confidence intervals (2{,}000 resamples, seed~42) throughout. Retrieval
quality is evaluated separately on 500 seeded dev queries against qrels, and
answer-level SPHP effects on 200 seeded queries.

\subsection{Leg Asymmetry and the TTFT Budget}
The two retrieval legs are asymmetric, but far less dramatically than
intuition suggests once the full deployment stack is measured: warm, the
sparse (BM25) leg averages 66--80~ms and the dense (BGE-M3 + HNSW) leg
186--194~ms---a $2.6\times$ ratio at top-$k{=}10$ through the full
gateway/gRPC path---while cold, the legs are 1{,}511--1{,}964~ms and
2{,}621--2{,}742~ms (a $1.6\times$ ratio; cold sparse is dominated by cold
index and page caches, not by BM25 itself). The dominant term of the TTFT
budget is neither leg: warm TTFT is $\approx$818~ms, of which retrieval
contributes only $\approx$190~ms and the \emph{prefill residual} (hydration +
engine prefill + queueing) contributes $\approx$630~ms. The practical
consequence inverts the naive reading of the warm asymmetry: because the two
legs run in parallel and are within a small factor of each other, no
placement can hide more than $\approx$100~ms of warm retrieval time behind
the other---while the prefill, the largest single warm term, is exactly the
component a speculative mechanism can attack
(\S\ref{sec:sphp}).

\subsection{Cache State Dominates; the Network Barely Matters}
The widest gap in Table~\ref{tab:latency} is vertical, not horizontal:
cold-prefix TTFT is $4.4\times$ warm TTFT at every regime
($\approx$3.5~s vs.\ $\approx$0.82~s). By contrast, TTFT is essentially flat
\emph{across} regimes: warm means move 817.9~$\rightarrow$~875.0~ms from
0~ms to 80~ms of RTT, a measured sensitivity of 0.73~ms of TTFT per ms of
RTT (Figure~\ref{fig:ttft}); the $+57$~ms at the 80~ms tier is consistent
with a single crossing of the link at identifier-sized payloads and is small
against both the warm TTFT and the within-state spread. Cold means are flat
within their wider confidence bands. Decode throughput is stable at
$\approx$35~tok/s (per-cell medians 34.4--35.7) across all regimes and
states, confirming that the link does not throttle the GPU-bound decode
phase. The systems conclusion is stark: for identifier-sized placement
boundaries, the network regime is a second-order term, and measurement
protocols that do not separate cache states will attribute cold-start cost
to whatever knob happens to be under study.

%  V. SPHP PROTOCOL
% ===========================================================================
\section{The SPHP Protocol}
\label{sec:sphp}

SPHP (Speculative Progressive Hydration \& Prefill) converts the largest
TTFT term---the prefill residual---into overlapped work. The key idea is to
begin the generation-side prefill \emph{before} the dense leg has finished,
using a cheap provisional context, and to reconcile once the true fused
context arrives.

\subsection{Provisional Sparse Hint}
As soon as the sparse leg completes at time $\Tsparse$, Node~B transmits a
\emph{provisional sparse hint}: the top-5 sparse document identifiers (a
$\approx$104-byte payload) over the link. This hint crosses the link in a
fraction of a millisecond at identifier payloads, and it is a legitimate, if
incomplete, approximation of the final fused ranking.

\subsection{Speculative Prefill with Masking}
On receiving the hint, Node~A immediately hydrates the five provisional
passages from local SQLite and begins a \emph{speculative prefill} of the
LLM on this provisional context. Crucially, the prefill is \emph{masked}: its
output is held in a scratch buffer and is \emph{not} emitted to the client.
The speculative prefill runs in parallel with the still-in-flight dense leg
(Figure~\ref{fig:sphp}).

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{figures/fig3_sphp_timeline.pdf}
\caption{SPHP timeline at RTT=40~ms, BW=100~Mbps, driven by the warm
campaign-fitted stage components. (a) Baseline P2 is fully serialized:
retrieval, fusion, transfer of the fused context, and prefill. (b) Under
SPHP, the provisional sparse hint triggers a masked speculative prefill that
overlaps the dense leg; on a hit, the first token is emitted at the later of
the fused-context arrival and the speculative-prefill completion. The
footnote reports the \emph{measured} TTFT deltas with bootstrap CIs.}
\label{fig:sphp}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{Reconciliation}
The dense leg completes at $\Tdense$ and the fused context (top-10
identifiers) arrives at Node~A at $t_{\mathrm{fused}}=\Tdense+\Tfusion+\Twan$.
SPHP then \emph{reconciles}. The implemented test is a set-overlap rule: the
speculation is declared a \emph{hit} when the provisional hint covers at
least half the hint set, i.e.,
$|\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{F}| \ge 0.5\,|\mathcal{H}|$, where
$\mathcal{H}$ is the top-5 sparse hint and $\mathcal{F}$ the fused top-10
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:sphp}). On a hit, the speculative prefill is kept and the
first token is emitted immediately---the provisional KV prefix is reused, so
the prefill is removed from the critical path. On a miss, the speculative
buffer is discarded and a standard P2 prefill runs on the true context; the
wasted provisional prefill is a \emph{measured} penalty $\Tmiss$
(193.6~ms warm, 2{,}697.8~ms cold in our campaign). With the \emph{measured}
hit rate $h=0.84$ (95\%~CI $[0.80,0.87]$; per-regime 0.82--0.90), the
expected TTFT is the hit-rate-weighted combination of the two cases.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{SPHP: Speculative Progressive Hydration \& Prefill}
\label{alg:sphp}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require query $q$; sparse leg $\Tsparse$; dense leg $\Tdense$;
WAN delay $\Twan$; hit threshold $\theta=0.5$
\State \textbf{Node B:} run sparse and dense legs in parallel
\State \textbf{Node B:} on sparse completion, send provisional hint
$\mathcal{H}$ (top-5 sparse ids) to Node A
\State \textbf{Node A:} hydrate $\mathcal{H}$; start \emph{masked}
speculative prefill $\hat{P}$
\State \textbf{Node B:} on dense completion, fuse (RRF) and send
$\mathcal{F}$ (top-10 ids) to Node A
\State \textbf{Node A:} on $\mathcal{F}$ arrival, reconcile
\If{$|\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{F}| \ge \theta\,|\mathcal{H}|$ \emph{(hit)}}
    \State keep $\hat{P}$; emit first token now
\Else \emph{(miss)}
    \State discard $\hat{P}$; run standard prefill on $\mathcal{F}$;
    pay $\Tmiss$
\EndIf
\State stream decoded tokens to the client
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Measured Effect and Answer-Level Cost}
Across the campaign, SPHP reduces cold-prefix TTFT by 17.4\%
(95\%~CI $[-20.1,-14.3]\%$) at every regime (e.g., 2{,}948~ms vs.\
3{,}634~ms at 0~ms RTT), while warm-state means differ by $-2.0\%$
(95\%~CI $[-5.1,+1.1]\%$)---within noise. The mechanism is visible in the
per-record instrumentation: on hits the wasted prefill is 0~ms (the
provisional KV prefix is kept), while misses pay $\Tmiss$; at $h=0.84$ the
expected value is favorable, and decisively so in the cold state where the
baseline prefill is largest.

Because a hit emits the answer from the \emph{sparse-only} provisional
context, we quantify the answer-level effect directly: for 200 seeded dev
queries we run the baseline and SPHP paths and compare their generated
answers. As a control, the miss arm re-generates from the \emph{identical}
fused context and measures pure decode stochasticity: its answers agree with
the baseline at token-F1 0.586 (EM 0.121)---the sampling-noise ceiling. The
hit-path answers agree at token-F1 0.422 (EM 0.042). We therefore do
\emph{not} claim answer parity: hit-path answers shift measurably beyond
sampling noise, because the provisional context omits the dense
contributions. The cost is bounded (comparable in magnitude to decode
stochasticity), the retrieved \emph{ranking} pipeline is untouched, and the
miss path is bit-identical to P2 by construction.

%  VI. ANALYTICAL PLACEMENT COST MODEL
% ===========================================================================
\section{Analytical Placement Cost Model}
\label{sec:costmodel}

We derive a closed-form cost model that predicts TTFT for each topology as a
function of the network regime, fit its parameters per cache state to the
campaign, and validate it at the regime level.

\subsection{WAN Transfer Term}
A one-way WAN transfer of a $p$-byte payload over a link with round-trip time
$\mathrm{rtt}$ and bandwidth $\mathrm{bw}$ (Mbps) is
\begin{equation}
\Twan(p,\mathrm{rtt},\mathrm{bw}) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{rtt}}{2}
\;+\; \frac{8\,p}{\mathrm{bw}\times 10^{6}}\times 10^{3}\ \text{ms}.
\label{eq:wan}
\end{equation}
The first term is propagation (half the RTT); the second is serialization.
Payload sizes are $p_{\mathrm{id}}=64+10\times8=144$~B (fused identifiers),
$p_{\mathrm{hint}}=64+5\times8=104$~B (sparse hint), and
$p_{\mathrm{text}}=64+5\times1200=6064$~B (hydrated passages).

\subsection{Topology Costs}
Let the retrieval phase be $T_{\mathrm{ret}} = \max(\Tsparse,\Tdense)$ (parallel legs) and let
hydration cost $\Thydrate$ for the top passages. The four topologies are:
\begin{align}
\text{P0:}\quad \Tttft^{(0)} &= T_{\mathrm{ret}} + \Tfusion + \Thydrate + \Tprefill,
\label{eq:p0}\\
\text{P2:}\quad \Tttft^{(2)} &= T_{\mathrm{ret}} + \Tfusion + \Thydrate \nonumber\\
&\quad + \Twan(p_{\mathrm{id}}) + \Tprefill,
\label{eq:p2}\\
\text{P3:}\quad \Tttft^{(3)} &= T_{\mathrm{ret}} + \Tfusion + \Thydrate \nonumber\\
&\quad + \Twan(p_{\mathrm{text}}) + \Tprefill,
\label{eq:p3}
\end{align}
where P0 is colocated, P2 ships only identifiers, and P3 ships full text.
For P2-SPHP, define the speculative-prefill start
$t_{s}=\Tsparse+\Twan(p_{\mathrm{hint}})+\Thydrate$, its completion
$t_{p}=t_{s}+\Tprefill$, and the fused-context arrival
$t_{f}=\Tdense+\Tfusion+\Twan(p_{\mathrm{id}})$. On a hit the first token is
emitted at $\max(t_{f},t_{p})$; on a miss it falls back to P2 plus the
measured wasted-prefill penalty $\Tmiss$. The expected TTFT is
\begin{equation}
\Tttft^{(2,\mathrm{SPHP})} =
h\max(t_{f},t_{p}) + (1-h)\bigl(\Tttft^{(2)}+\Tmiss\bigr).
\label{eq:sphp}
\end{equation}
The total end-to-end latency adds decode and token-streaming time:
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{e2e}} \;=\; \Tttft \;+\; \frac{N_{\mathrm{tok}}}{\Rdecode}\times10^{3}
\;+\; \Twan(N_{\mathrm{tok}}\times4).
\label{eq:e2e}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Per-State Fitting and Regime-Level Validation}
Every parameter is fitted from the campaign separately per cache state,
because the components differ by an order of magnitude between them: warm
$\Tsparse{=}74.8$~ms, $\Tdense{=}190.8$~ms, $\Tprefill{=}633.3$~ms (the
residual after measured retrieval and fusion); cold 1{,}711.3, 2{,}680.9, and
850.7~ms respectively; $\Rdecode{=}35.2$~tok/s;
$h{=}0.84$ and $\Tmiss$ measured as above. \emph{Why regime-level
validation:} query-level TTFT variance is dominated by prompt content rather
than placement, so a population-mean predictor cannot and should not be
scored per query (a per-query protocol yields a negative $R^2$ for
\emph{any} mean predictor, ours included). The decision a placement policy
actually makes is \emph{per regime}, so we validate exactly that:
leave-one-tier-out, fitting all parameters on three RTT tiers and predicting
the held-out tier's mean. The macro P2 model achieves \textbf{1.28\%} (cold)
and \textbf{2.76\%} (warm) held-out regime MAPE; the SPHP expected-value
model 10.6\% and 5.8\%. As a form-error diagnostic, the component-level
check---which substitutes the observed per-record retrieval time and scores
only the network, hydration, and prefill terms---yields 3.0\% (cold) and
11.3\% (warm) MAPE, and the SPHP \emph{hit-branch} form check 7.4\% (cold) /
14.8\% (warm) (Figure~\ref{fig:parity}).

\includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{figures/fig8_cost_model_parity.pdf}
\caption{Regime-level cost-model parity: predicted vs.\ observed per-tier

\subsection{Crossover Analysis}
To evaluate where physical pipeline boundaries matter, we sweep the fitted
model over RTT $\in[0,200]$~ms and bandwidth $\in[10,1000]$~Mbps for both
cache states (Figure~\ref{fig:crossover}). The result is uniform: the
SPHP-augmented split placement (P2-SPHP) is optimal in \emph{every} cell of
the sweep, with a modeled warm-state advantage of 7--8\% that is essentially
independent of both RTT and bandwidth, because the hint and fused-context
payloads (104 and 144~B) serialize in well under a millisecond at any
practical bandwidth. P3 is indistinguishable from P2 for TTFT at
$\ge$10~Mbps (within 5~ms---six kilobytes of hydrated text barely matters);
its serialization penalty only becomes visible under bandwidth starvation
($\approx$96~ms at 0.5~Mbps, Figure~\ref{fig:crossover}(b)). We note
honestly where the closed form diverges from measurement: the model's
expected-value form \emph{underestimates} the measured cold-state SPHP
benefit (7.8\% modeled vs.\ 17.4\% measured) because a hit also preserves
the provisional KV prefix---a credit the closed form does not
encode---while the measured warm-state advantage is within noise where the
model predicts a small positive effect. The model is therefore a
conservative lower bound on the cold-start benefit and an optimistic upper
bound on the warm-state one.

\subsection{TTFT versus RTT: Measured}

\subsection{Retrieval Quality}
A placement or speculative mechanism is only acceptable if it does not
degrade retrieval quality. Figure~\ref{fig:quality} reports retrieval metrics
computed live on the deployed Node~B gateway over \emph{500 seeded} MS~MARCO
dev queries (seed~42; top-$k{=}100$, RRF $k{=}60$). Dense retrieval (BGE-M3)
is the precision champion (MRR@10 0.321, nDCG@10 0.377), while Hybrid (RRF)
is the coverage champion: Recall@100 0.851 (vs.\ 0.848 dense, 0.669 sparse)
and Recall@1000 \textbf{0.895} (vs.\ 0.848 dense---the sparse leg is capped
at $k{=}100$ and cannot extend). Sparse BM25 is weakest on every metric.
These numbers ground the hybrid design: RRF buys a small top-rank precision
discount for a material recall margin.

\subsection{Throughput}
Decode throughput is stable at $\approx$35~tok/s (per-cell medians
34.4--35.7~tok/s; Table~\ref{tab:latency}) across all regimes and cache
states, confirming that the link does not throttle the GPU-bound decode
phase and that SPHP's speculative prefill does not contend with decode for
GPU resources. Warm end-to-end totals of 1.6--2.0~s against TTFT of
$\approx$0.82~s reflect the decode phase's share of the budget, which
placement---and SPHP---leave intact.

\subsection{Live Policy Demonstration}
The cost model is not only retrospective: at deployment time it reads the
fitted parameters, predicts P2 and P2-SPHP TTFT for the \emph{current}
network regime, and selects the argmin placement. We demonstrate this live:
under each netem regime the policy selects a placement, executes live
queries, and logs the predicted versus realized TTFT. In the demonstration
the policy selects P2-SPHP at \emph{every} regime, consistent with the
offline crossover analysis. Each regime serves five fresh seed-42 queries
three times back-to-back (repeat~0 is the cold-prefix reference; repeats
1--2 are warm), after two unrecorded warmups, at $k{=}10$; selection uses
the steady-state (warm) fit.

Table~\ref{tab:livepolicy} reports the outcome. In the warm state the
realized mean TTFT of the selected placement tracks its prediction to
within 4.5--8.6\% across all four regimes---inside the 15\% tolerance
RQ~3 asks of the policy, and comparable to the regime-level validation
errors of the fit itself. In the cold state the model \emph{overestimates}
absolute TTFT by 25--36\%: fresh contexts in the demo realized
2.1--2.4~s against cold predictions of $\approx$3.3~s. The error is
conservative and leaves the argmin unchanged (the cold fit also selects
P2-SPHP), but its direction is informative: the campaign's cold reference
carries the prefix-cache pressure of a sustained 1{,}200-request run,
whereas the demo's lighter history evicts less, so realized cold cost
lands below the fitted worst case. The live run therefore re-confirms the
paper's central operational finding from the policy side: a single-shot
deployment serving only fresh contexts runs at $\approx$3$\times$
steady-state TTFT regardless of placement, and a placement policy must
condition on cache state---which the fitted model carries as explicit
per-state parameters.

\subsection{Summary of Gains}
SPHP reduces cold-prefix TTFT by 17.4\% (95\%~CI $[-20.1,-14.3]\%$)
consistently across all four regimes, at a measured hit rate of 0.84; warm
TTFT is unchanged within noise. The saving is insensitive to both RTT and
bandwidth because the extra protocol message is 104 bytes and the
speculation targets the prefill, not the network. The mechanism costs one
small extra message, a masked prefill that is discarded only on a miss
($\Tmiss{=}194$~ms warm, 2{,}698~ms cold, measured), and a bounded
answer-level shift on the hit path (\S\ref{sec:sphp}).

%  VIII. DISCUSSION
% ===========================================================================
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Cache state and prefill---not RTT---are the design drivers.} Our
central empirical results invert the folklore that WAN placement is mainly
about network delay: with identifier-sized boundaries, TTFT is flat across
0--80~ms while cache state swings TTFT by $4.4\times$. Placement decisions
for hybrid RAG should therefore be made from a cache-state-aware measurement
matrix, and deployments should spend their engineering budget on keeping
prefixes warm (or making cold starts cheap, which is precisely SPHP's
shape) rather than on micro-optimizing the link.

\textbf{A simple, robust policy.} The crossover analysis's uniformity---P2-SPHP
optimal in every swept cell---is a practically important result: a deployer
need not re-solve placement per network regime. A single SPHP-augmented
split placement is a safe default across the (RTT, bandwidth) space we
studied, and the regime-validated cost model (\S\ref{sec:costmodel})
provides the analytical basis---and the live policy hook---for extending the
policy to new regimes.

\textbf{Threats to validity.}
\emph{Hardware confound:} the dense leg runs on a laptop-class GTX~1660~Ti,
so absolute leg times (and the $2.6\times$ warm asymmetry) are
hardware-specific; the ratios we report are qualified by $k$ and by the
measurement path, and the qualitative conclusions (prefill dominance, cache
dominance, RTT flatness) are robust to plausible hardware changes.
\emph{Emulation fidelity:} \texttt{netem} shapes delay bidirectionally but
with zero loss, no jitter, no reordering, and no route asymmetry; genuine
WAN validation is future work (\S\ref{sec:roadmap}).
\emph{Single dataset and generator:} results are MS~MARCO-specific (verbose
web queries) and tied to one W4A16-quantized 27B generator with speculative
decoding enabled; the decode rate ($\approx$35~tok/s) and prefill residual
are model-specific. SPHP's hit rate is a property of the sparse--dense
overlap distribution and may differ on other corpora or embedders.
\emph{No concurrency:} all measurements are single-query streams; queueing
under concurrent load is out of scope.
\emph{Cold-state semantics:} repeat~0 is a cold-\emph{prefix} reference
under a resident engine, not a cold process start; we report it explicitly
rather than conflating the two.

\textbf{Relation to layer- and phase-level partitioning.} Neurosurgeon
\cite{kang2017neurosurgeon} and its successors partition a single neural
network across a device and a server at the \emph{layer} boundary, and LLM
serving systems such as Splitwise partition a model's \emph{phases}
(prefill vs.\ decode) across machines. Our setting is different in two ways.
First, the unit of placement is a \emph{pipeline stage} of a RAG system
(sparse, dense, fusion, hydration, generation), not a layer or a phase of one
model, so the stages have heterogeneous and independently scalable resource
profiles. Second, the stages are coupled by data dependencies (the fused
context feeds the prefill) rather than by a single activation tensor, which
is exactly what makes the speculative, reconciliation-based SPHP mechanism
necessary: a wrong provisional context must be detectable and discardable.
Extending speculation across more than two stages multiplies the
reconciliation surface: each additional provisional context turns the hit
test into a set-membership check over a larger provisional family, and
misses compound---a direction we discuss below.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} We release the full 8.84M-passage index, the
benchmark harness (campaign driver, netem control, seeded query sampling),
the scoring pipeline (retrieval-quality and answer-level comparison
scripts), the cost model and its refit, and all figure-generating code and
run files as open artifacts (C4). Every number in this paper regenerates
from committed scripts with the seeds disclosed above.

\subsection{Future Work and Three-Node Validation Roadmap}
\label{sec:roadmap}
Three primary avenues build directly upon this foundation. First, a
\emph{three-node physical WAN campaign}: a third node enrolls as a
\emph{sparse-retrieval and user-facing tier}---serving the client directly
while offloading the Tantivy index---so that the sparse hint originates one
further hop away and the placement taxonomy gains a genuinely three-way
split; executing it over a public cross-site route will validate our netem
predictions under genuine Internet jitter, loss, and reordering. Second,
quantifying \emph{graceful degradation}: measuring latency and quality
impacts when an edge node experiences transient disconnects and pipeline
stages consolidate. Third, generalizing SPHP beyond sparse--dense pairs to
multi-stage speculative prefetching across distributed multi-hop topologies,
where the reconciliation test becomes a set-membership check over a larger
provisional set.

% ===========================================================================
%  IX. CONCLUSION
% ===========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a cache-state-aware measurement study of stage-to-node
placement for a hybrid RAG pipeline on commodity heterogeneous hardware over
a controlled WAN. The measurements reorder the design space: TTFT is flat
across 0--80~ms of RTT (0.73~ms/ms), cache state dominates variance
($4.4\times$), and the prefill residual is the largest warm TTFT term. SPHP
exploits exactly that structure---a provisional sparse hint, a masked
speculative prefill overlapping the dense leg, and a set-overlap
reconciliation---cutting measured cold-prefix TTFT by 17.4\% at every
regime at a measured hit rate of 0.84, with the answer-level cost bounded
and disclosed. An analytical placement cost model, validated at the regime
level (1.3--2.8\% leave-one-tier-out MAPE), shows the SPHP-augmented split
placement is optimal across the entire swept (RTT, bandwidth) space. These
results give practitioners a measured, model-based answer to where each
stage of a hybrid RAG pipeline should run, and a reproducible testbed to
extend the study to larger fleets and genuine wide-area conditions.
