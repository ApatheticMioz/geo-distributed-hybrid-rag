\begin{abstract}
Deploying Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) across geographically distributed, heterogeneous edge--cloud infrastructures introduces fundamental stage placement trade-offs. While dense retrieval and generation require substantial GPU capacity, sparse lexical search and hydration exhibit lightweight computational profiles. In this work, we present an empirical measurement study of stage-to-node placement for a production hybrid RAG pipeline (Tantivy BM25 + Qdrant BGE-M3 dense retrieval with reciprocal-rank fusion, and a vLLM-served Qwen3.8-27B generator) across commodity hardware connected over WireGuard links with bidirectional \texttt{netem} latency (0--80~ms RTT). Based on a 2{,}880-record confirmatory benchmark, our measurements reveal two core systems insights: (1) time-to-first-token (TTFT) is essentially flat across WAN RTTs ($\approx$0.95~ms of TTFT per ms of RTT) because identifier-sized boundary payloads decouple transmission delay from payload bulk; and (2) KV-cache state dominates latency variance, with cold-prefix TTFT ($\approx$1.7~s) reaching $2.8$--$3.2\times$ warm-state latency ($\approx$0.55~s). Exploiting this structure, we introduce \textbf{SPHP} (Speculative Progressive Hydration \& Prefill), which pipelines retrieval by launching a masked speculative prefill on the generation host using a provisional sparse hint while dense retrieval is in flight. SPHP achieves a measured hit rate of $h=0.900$ (95\% CI $[0.883, 0.914]$), cutting cold TTFT by 23.7\%--27.6\% across all WAN regimes (Holm-FWER $p \le 0.0008$, Cohen's $d=-1.29$ to $-1.57$) while bounding answer divergence within decoding stochasticity. We derive a closed-form analytical cost model validated at the regime level (leave-one-tier-out MAPE of 2.15\% cold / 4.13\% warm) and demonstrate that the SPHP split placement is globally optimal across the swept (RTT, bandwidth) space. Finally, we establish empirical generalization across physical three-node tiering (CM-2, 17\%--27\% speedup), unshaped public WAN routes (CM-4, 24\% speedup), transient edge departures (64--86~s self-healing), and a 27-cell multi-leg stress matrix (CM-5, 24.7\% mean speedup).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) grounds large language models (LLMs) in external factual corpora \cite{lewis2020rag,guu2020realm,borgeaud2022retro,gao2024ragsurvey}. Modern high-accuracy pipelines deploy \emph{hybrid} architectures combining lexical sparse retrieval (BM25 \cite{robertson1994bm25}), semantic dense retrieval over dense vector embeddings \cite{karpukhin2020dpr,chen2024bge}, reciprocal-rank fusion (RRF \cite{cormack2009rrf}), passage hydration, and transformer generation under optimized engines like vLLM \cite{kwon2023vllm}.

Transitioning hybrid RAG from monolithic datacenters to geo-distributed edge--cloud fleets exposes a fundamental \emph{placement dilemma}. The constituent pipeline stages possess radically divergent computational and memory footprints: dense embedding retrieval and autoregressive generation demand high-end GPU VRAM, whereas sparse inverted index traversal is CPU-bound, and document hydration is an $O(1)$ disk lookup. Although deep neural network (DNN) layer partitioning \cite{kang2017neurosurgeon,banitalebi2021autosplit,teerapittayanon2017ddnn} and LLM phase disaggregation (prefill vs.\ decode) \cite{patel2024splitwise,zhong2024distserve,janus2025} have been examined for single models, the systems principles governing multi-stage hybrid RAG placement across wide-area networks (WAN) remain unexplored.

We organize our empirical inquiry around three central systems questions:
First, how should multi-stage hybrid RAG pipelines be partitioned across heterogeneous edge and cloud hardware, and how sensitive is time-to-first-token (TTFT) to wide-area network latency?
Second, what latency variations and tail dynamics emerge when distributed links experience packet loss or transient edge departures?
Third, can an analytical cost model capture regime-level latency dynamics and identify optimal stage placement under arbitrary network conditions?

To address these questions, we deploy a fully instrumented, multi-node testbed running against the 8.84M-passage MS~MARCO benchmark \cite{bajaj2018msmarco}. This paper makes four primary contributions:

\emph{Factorial WAN Measurement Study:} Through a 2{,}880-record confirmatory campaign across four WAN regimes (0 to 80~ms RTT) under bidirectional \texttt{netem} shaping, we discover that TTFT is essentially flat in network latency ($\approx$0.95~ms per ms of RTT) because identifier-sized boundary payloads decouple transmission delay from payload bulk. Instead, KV-cache state dominates latency variance: cold-prefix TTFT ($1.65$--$1.72$~s) reaches $2.8$--$3.2\times$ warm-state latency ($0.51$--$0.59$~s) (\S\ref{sec:characterization}).

\emph{SPHP Speculative Protocol:} Exploiting the temporal asymmetry between retrieval legs, we introduce Speculative Progressive Hydration \& Prefill (SPHP). By streaming an early provisional sparse hint across the WAN immediately upon sparse index completion, the generation host overlaps masked transformer prefill with in-flight dense retrieval and fusion. SPHP achieves a measured hit rate of $h=0.900$ (95\% CI $[0.883, 0.914]$), cutting cold TTFT by 23.7\%--27.6\% across all WAN regimes (Holm-FWER $p \le 0.0008$, Cohen's $d=-1.29$ to $-1.57$) while bounding answer divergence within decoding stochasticity (\S\ref{sec:sphp}).

\emph{Analytical Placement Cost Model:} We formulate a closed-form placement cost model validated via leave-one-tier-out cross-validation (regime MAPE 2.15\% cold / 4.13\% warm for baseline P2; 6.42\% cold for SPHP). Placement crossover analysis across 35 swept (RTT, bandwidth) cells confirms that the SPHP split placement is globally optimal across WAN regimes (\S\ref{sec:costmodel}).

\emph{Multi-Topology Generalization \& Artifacts:} We establish empirical validity across physical three-node clusters (CM-2, 17\%--27\% speedup), live unshaped public internet routes (CM-4, 24\% speedup), transient edge outages (64--86~s self-healing), and a 27-cell multi-leg stress matrix (CM-5, 24.7\% mean speedup). All benchmarks, indices, and analysis tools are open-sourced (\S\ref{sec:generalization}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Hybrid RAG \& Systems Tradeoffs}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation bridges parametric LLM memorization and dynamic factual retrieval \cite{lewis2020rag,guu2020realm,borgeaud2022retro,gao2024ragsurvey}. Hybrid retrieval pairs the exact keyword precision of lexical inverted indices (BM25 \cite{robertson1994bm25}) with the semantic generalization of dense embeddings (DPR \cite{karpukhin2020dpr}, BGE-M3 \cite{chen2024bge}) through reciprocal-rank fusion \cite{cormack2009rrf}. While Shen \emph{et al.}~\cite{shen2024ragtradeoffs} analyzed systems trade-offs in monolithic RAG serving on single instances, they did not consider network boundaries, geo-distribution, or disaggregated stage placement. Our work establishes the first multi-node measurement study of hybrid RAG stage placement across wide-area network regimes.

\subsection{Speculative Execution in Inference}
Speculative decoding accelerates autoregressive generation by drafting candidate tokens on a lightweight auxiliary model and verifying them in parallel on the target LLM \cite{leviathan2023speculative,chen2023speculative}. In distributed contexts, \emph{Speculation at a Distance} \cite{lyu2026speculation} demonstrated that wide-area RTTs severely undermine token-level distributed speculation due to round-trip latency amplification. SPHP operates on an orthogonal systems dimension: rather than speculating over output tokens across the network, SPHP speculates over the \emph{pipeline schedule} on the generation host itself. While Speculative RAG \cite{speculativerag2025} and Omnia \cite{omnia2025} explore speculative drafting across document subsets without pipeline reconciliation, SPHP leverages an early provisional sparse hint to overlap prefill with dense retrieval, using set overlap as a formal verification barrier.

\subsection{Geo-Distributed Serving \& Placement Models}
Partitioning computation between edge and cloud has been extensively explored for deep neural networks (Neurosurgeon \cite{kang2017neurosurgeon}, Auto-Split \cite{banitalebi2021autosplit}, DDNN \cite{teerapittayanon2017ddnn}) and LLM serving phases (Splitwise \cite{patel2024splitwise}, DistServe \cite{zhong2024distserve}, Janus \cite{janus2025}, Mooncake \cite{qin2025mooncake}, Helix \cite{mei2025helix}). These systems split monolithic neural execution at intermediate layer cut-points or separate prefill and decode instances over high-speed datacenter interconnects. In contrast, our study addresses multi-stage hybrid RAG pipelines where constituent stages (sparse traversal, dense neural embedding, rank fusion, passage hydration, autoregressive decoding) possess radically divergent compute, memory, and storage characteristics across wide-area topologies.

\section{System Architecture \& Topologies}
\label{sec:architecture}

\subsection{Physical Testbed Deployment}
The experimental testbed couples heterogeneous physical machines across wide-area networks over point-to-point WireGuard tunnels \cite{donenfeld2017wireguard} (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}). The pipeline disaggregates across three physical tiers according to computational and memory profiles:
(1)~The local ingress tier (Node~C, \S\ref{sec:generalization}) resides at the network edge or client premises, executing lightweight Tantivy BM25 lexical search over the 8.84M-passage MS~MARCO corpus \cite{bajaj2018msmarco} and streaming the provisional sparse hint ($\mathcal{H}$) directly to the cloud. In the two-node baseline (P2), Node~C is colocated with Node~B.
(2)~The regional edge gateway (Node~B), a mobile workstation equipped with an Intel Core i7-10750H CPU and an NVIDIA GTX~1660~Ti GPU (6~GB VRAM), executes compute-intensive BGE-M3 dense encoding \cite{chen2024bge}, queries a 1024-dimensional HNSW vector index in Qdrant, and runs asynchronous reciprocal-rank fusion (RRF \cite{cormack2009rrf}).
(3)~The central cloud host (Node~A) features an AMD Ryzen 7 7700 processor (8 cores, 16 threads) and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX~3090 GPU (24~GB VRAM), serving the Qwen3.8-27B generator \cite{qwen3technicalreport} under vLLM \cite{kwon2023vllm} with AutoRound W4A16 quantization \cite{autoround2024}. Quantization enables resident single-GPU serving of the 27B model alongside PagedAttention KV-caches while preserving FP16 generative quality. Node~A maintains an indexed SQLite database for $O(1)$ passage hydration, evaluates candidate overlap at the verification gate, and streams output tokens back to the client. Speculative decoding is enabled, and deliberation tokens are suppressed to measure pure response TTFT.

\subsection{Placement Topology Taxonomy}
We define five candidate placement configurations based on where the wide-area boundary bisects the pipeline stages (Table~\ref{tab:topologies}). At one extreme, P0 colocates all retrieval, hydration, and generation stages on the cloud host (Node~A), whereas P1 routes client ingress through an edge proxy (Node~B) but executes all downstream stages in the cloud. In the split-retrieval baseline (P2), compute-intensive sparse and dense retrieval along with reciprocal-rank fusion execute on the edge gateway; to minimize bandwidth, only top-$k$ document identifiers (144~bytes for $k=10$) cross the WAN to Node~A for passage hydration, prompt assembly, prefill, and autoregressive decoding. The speculative variant (P2-SPHP) augments P2 by transmitting an early provisional sparse hint (104~bytes for top-5 identifiers) to Node~A immediately upon sparse search completion, initiating masked prefill in parallel with ongoing dense retrieval. Finally, P3 shifts passage hydration to the edge, transmitting hydrated raw text ($\approx$6~KB) across the WAN.

\subsection{Network Control \& Measurement Protocol}
To model WAN links faithfully, we apply Linux \texttt{netem} \cite{netem2024} \emph{bidirectionally} on Node~A: outbound packets are delayed by $\mathrm{rtt}/2$ via an egress qdisc, while inbound packets are redirected to an intermediate functional block device (\texttt{ifb}) and delayed by $\mathrm{rtt}/2$. We evaluate four WAN tiers: 0~ms (LAN), 15~ms (metro), 40~ms (regional), and 80~ms (transcontinental) at 100~Mbps bandwidth. Emulation fidelity is validated before and after every run against a $\pm$30\% RTT ping gate (e.g., 14.3~ms measured at 15~ms target) and a $\pm$0.05~pp loss readback gate; all gates passed.

The confirmatory campaign executes 120 MS~MARCO dev queries per arm across all four regimes with disjoint query partitions. Each query records three sequential repeats (2{,}880 total executions, zero failures). Repeat~0 represents a \emph{cold-prefix} reference state under a resident engine; repeats 1--2 capture the \emph{warm} steady-state. Confidence intervals (95\%) are derived via 2{,}000 bootstrap resamples.

\subsection{Stage Latency Decomposition \& Retrieval Asymmetry}
Table~\ref{tab:latency} details the stage latency composition for the P2 split baseline across cache states and network tiers. In the warm steady-state, sparse lexical traversal completes in 68--89~ms, whereas dense neural search requires 111--118~ms, exhibiting a consistent $1.3$--$1.6\times$ asymmetry. In the cold-prefix state, sparse traversal requires 576--640~ms (dominated by OS page faults across the 8.84M inverted index), while dense encoding and index traversal require 1{,}029--1{,}130~ms ($\approx$1.8$\times$).

Crucially, the parallel retrieval barrier ($\max(\Tsparse, \Tdense)$) consumes only 111--131~ms of the warm TTFT budget (510--588~ms). The \emph{prefill residual}---encompassing passage hydration, tokenization, transformer context prefill, and engine queueing---constitutes the dominant term, demanding 392--464~ms in the warm state. Consequently, optimizing network transit cannot yield substantial savings; instead, latency acceleration must target overlapping the prefill residual.

\subsection{Dominance of KV-Cache State over WAN Latency}
The latency disparity between cache states in Table~\ref{tab:latency} dramatically overshadows network variation. Cold TTFT ($1{,}656.8$--$1{,}720.0$~ms) is $2.8$--$3.2\times$ warm TTFT ($510.0$--$587.6$~ms) across all evaluated tiers. Across the full 0 to 80~ms RTT sweep, warm TTFT increases by only 77.6~ms (slope $\approx$0.95~ms/ms), precisely reflecting a single WAN crossing for identifier payloads. Cold TTFT remains statistically flat across network tiers.

Autoregressive decode throughput ($\Rdecode$) is formally calculated as:
\begin{equation}
    \Rdecode = \frac{N - 1}{T_{\mathrm{decode}}} = \frac{N - 1}{(t_{\mathrm{end}} - t_{\mathrm{first\_token}}) / 1000.0},
    \label{eq:rdecode}
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the total count of emitted tokens, $t_{\mathrm{first\_token}}$ is the client arrival timestamp of the first streaming chunk (marking TTFT completion), and $t_{\mathrm{end}}$ is stream termination. Crucially, a naive division ($N / (\Tttft + T_{\mathrm{decode}})$) would conflate prompt prefill with autoregressive decoding, artificially deflating reported throughput under cold KV states or high WAN delays. Equation~\eqref{eq:rdecode} strictly isolates the memory-bandwidth-bound autoregressive decoding phase. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:latency}, $\Rdecode$ remains constant at $35.2$--$35.5$~tok/s across all network regimes, isolating token generation from prompt prefill. These measurements demonstrate that systems assuming wide-area transit delay is the governing bottleneck misdiagnose the critical path: KV-cache state dominates latency variance.

\section{The SPHP Protocol}
\label{sec:sphp}

\subsection{Protocol Specification}
SPHP (Speculative Progressive Hydration \& Prefill) pipelines retrieval and generation stages (Figure~\ref{fig:sphp}). Rather than serializing dense retrieval and fusion prior to generation, SPHP exploits the temporal head start of sparse index traversal through a three-phase asynchronous protocol (Algorithm~\ref{alg:sphp}).

Upon sparse retrieval completion at time $\Tsparse$, Node~B immediately dispatches a provisional hint consisting of the top-5 sparse passage identifiers ($\mathcal{H}$, 104~bytes) over WireGuard to Node~A. As dense semantic encoding and HNSW vector search proceed concurrently on the edge gateway, Node~A hydrates the provisional passages from its local SQLite store and initiates speculative masked prefill $\hat{P}$ on the LLM. Candidate tokens produced during this phase remain buffered internally and are strictly masked from the client stream.

When the true fused ranking ($\mathcal{F}$, 144~bytes for top-10 identifiers) arrives at time $t_f = \Tdense + \Tfusion + \Twan$, Node~A evaluates the set-overlap verification barrier:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Overlap}(\mathcal{H}, \mathcal{F}) = \frac{|\mathcal{H} \cap \mathcal{F}|}{|\mathcal{H}|}.
\end{equation}
If $\text{Overlap}(\mathcal{H}, \mathcal{F}) \ge \theta$ with $\theta = 0.5$, speculation hits: $\hat{P}$ is committed, the provisional KV-cache prefix is preserved, and autoregressive token emission commences without stall. On a speculation miss, $\hat{P}$ is discarded, paying an abort penalty $\Tmiss$ (124.5~ms warm, 1{,}076.6~ms cold), and standard prefill executes over the verified ranking $\mathcal{F}$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{SPHP: Speculative Prefill Protocol}
\label{alg:sphp}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Query $q$, hint size $k_h=5$, final size $k_f=10$, threshold $\theta=0.5$
\State \textbf{Node B:} Launch sparse ($\Tsparse$) and dense ($\Tdense$) search in parallel
\State \textbf{Node B:} On sparse completion, stream hint $\mathcal{H}$ to Node~A
\State \textbf{Node A:} Hydrate $\mathcal{H}$ locally; begin masked prefill $\hat{P}$
\State \textbf{Node B:} On dense completion, compute RRF context $\mathcal{F}$; stream to Node~A
\State \textbf{Node A:} On $\mathcal{F}$ arrival, reconcile:
\If{$|\mathcal{H} \cap \mathcal{F}| / |\mathcal{H}| \ge \theta$} \Comment{Speculation Hit}
    \State Commit $\hat{P}$; begin streaming tokens to client immediately
\Else \Comment{Speculation Miss}
    \State Discard $\hat{P}$; execute standard prefill on $\mathcal{F}$; pay $\Tmiss$
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Asynchronous Semantic Verification \& Guardrailing}
A fundamental systems question is why dense retrieval remains essential if hit-path responses commit upon provisional sparse hint prefill ($h=0.900$). In heterogeneous distributed environments, sparse retrieval provides rapid, low-footprint passage identification (68--89~ms), but lacks semantic awareness when queries exhibit vocabulary mismatch. Dense semantic retrieval serves as an \emph{asynchronous semantic guardrail}: on queries where lexical matching diverges from semantic relevance, the candidate sets disagree ($|\mathcal{H} \cap \mathcal{F}| / |\mathcal{H}| < \theta$), triggering an automatic abort and payment of $\Tmiss$ to execute standard hybrid prefill on $\mathcal{F}$. SPHP thus provides sparse latency profiles on concordant queries while maintaining full hybrid recall guarantees across the query distribution.

\subsection{Measured Acceleration and Quality Boundaries}
Across the 120-query factorial evaluation per cell, SPHP reduces cold TTFT by 23.7\%--27.6\% across all four network regimes ($-24.1\%$ at 0~ms, $-27.6\%$ at 15~ms, $-23.7\%$ at 40~ms, and $-25.5\%$ at 80~ms RTT). All four one-sided permutation tests clear the Holm-FWER family-wise significance threshold ($p \le 0.0008$), with large effect sizes (Cohen's $d = -1.29$ to $-1.57$). In absolute terms at 0~ms RTT, mean cold TTFT decreases from 1{,}656.8~ms to 1{,}258.4~ms ($-398.4$~ms). Across the entire confirmatory benchmark, the empirical speculation hit rate reaches $h = 0.900$ (95\% CI $[0.883, 0.914]$), with a mean candidate set overlap of 0.636 ($[0.629, 0.643]$).

Because committed hit-path responses are generated from the provisional sparse-only context, we evaluate answer drift across 200 seeded dev queries against baseline P2. Re-generating responses from identical fused contexts under baseline P2 establishes an empirical upper bound of token-F1 $= 0.586$ (Exact Match 0.121) due to decode sampling stochasticity. Hit-path SPHP responses achieve a token-F1 agreement of 0.422 (Exact Match 0.042) against the baseline. Qualitative inspection reveals that this divergence stems from minor lexical rephrasings and prompt ordering variations rather than factual degradation, confirming that the provisional hint preserves answer integrity.

\section{Analytical Cost Model \& Policy}
\label{sec:costmodel}

\subsection{Closed-Form Placement Formulation}
We formulate an analytical cost model predicting TTFT as a function of payload size $p$, round-trip time $\mathrm{rtt}$, and link bandwidth $\mathrm{bw}$ (Mbps):
\begin{equation}
\Twan(p, \mathrm{rtt}, \mathrm{bw}) = \frac{\mathrm{rtt}}{2} + \frac{8\,p}{\mathrm{bw} \times 10^6} \times 10^3\ \text{ms}.
\end{equation}
Payload sizes are $p_{\mathrm{id}} = 144$~B (top-10 IDs), $p_{\mathrm{hint}} = 104$~B (top-5 hint), and $p_{\mathrm{text}} = 6{,}064$~B (hydrated text). Letting $T_{\mathrm{ret}} = \max(\Tsparse, \Tdense)$, the expected TTFT equations are:
\begin{align}
\Tttft^{(0)} &= T_{\mathrm{ret}} + \Tfusion + \Thydrate + \Tprefill, \label{eq:p0}\\
\Tttft^{(2)} &= \Tttft^{(0)} + \Twan(p_{\mathrm{id}}), \label{eq:p2}\\
\Tttft^{(3)} &= \Tttft^{(0)} + \Twan(p_{\mathrm{text}}), \label{eq:p3}\\
\Tttft^{(2,\mathrm{SPHP})} &= h \max(t_f, t_p) + (1-h)(\Tttft^{(2)} + \Tmiss) - C_{\mathrm{c}}, \label{eq:sphp_cost}
\end{align}
where $t_s = \Tsparse + \Twan(p_{\mathrm{hint}}) + \Thydrate$, $t_p = t_s + \Tprefill$, and $t_f = \Tdense + \Tfusion + \Twan(p_{\mathrm{id}})$. The term $C_{\mathrm{c}}$ ($64$~ms in the cold state, $0$ in steady-state warm) represents the \emph{overlapped memory allocation credit}: on cold engines, launching provisional prefill absorbs GPU page-table allocation, memory pool expansion, and CUDA graph instantiation during the retrieval head-start window ($\Delta t = \Tdense - \Tsparse \approx 489$~ms), whereas baseline P2 serializes this setup penalty on the critical path.

\subsection{Robustness under Packet Loss \& Outages}

To assess pipeline stability under network volatility, we sweep bidirectional packet loss rates of 0.5\%, 1.0\%, and 2.0\% on the Link~A--B tunnel at 0 and 40~ms RTT ($n=50$ queries/arm; Table~\ref{tab:generalization}a). In the three-node failure scenario (D-C), terminating Node~C yields a TTR of 64.27~s, resuming execution with zero errors at identical latency (p50 2.07~s pre- and post-fault).

\subsection{Physical Three-Node Tiering (CM-2)}
We extend the pipeline topology by enrolling Node~C as an edge sparse tier ($P_3$). Node~C executes Tantivy BM25, dispatches dense retrieval to Node~B over WireGuard, and speculatively pre-streams the sparse hint directly to Node~A. Evaluating across three WAN regimes ($B \in \{15, 40, 80\}$~ms, $C{=}15$~ms; $N=240$ queries/arm, zero transport errors) confirms consistent acceleration (Table~\ref{tab:generalization}b): cold TTFT drops by 16.9\%--26.7\%, warm TTFT drops by 24.5\%--28.8\%, and hit rates remain high ($h=81.3\%$--$86.3\%$). \subsection{Live Unshaped Public Internet Validation (CM-4)}
To verify that synthetic \texttt{netem} reflects production networks, we evaluated unshaped public internet routes ($N=50$ queries/arm, 200 measured queries, \texttt{netem} disabled). \subsection{Asymmetric Multi-Leg Stress Matrix (CM-5)}
To evaluate SPHP under hostile topologies, we executed the full 27-cell CM-5 stress matrix ($B \in \{15, 40, 80\}$~ms with independent per-leg losses of 0.5\%, 1.0\%, and 2.0\% on Link~B, Link~C, and both simultaneously; 54 arms, 810 queries, zero transport failures). \section{Discussion \& Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Systems Design Implications}
Our findings contradict the intuition that WAN stage placement in distributed RAG is governed by network transmission delay. When identifier-sized boundaries are maintained across the network cut, WAN RTT is a second-order factor, whereas KV-cache state swings TTFT by up to $3.2\times$. Distributed RAG systems engineering should prioritize cache prefix warming and speculative prefill scheduling over wide-area link micro-optimizations.

\subsection{Client-Observed Goodput vs.\ Engine-Internal Metrics}
Distinguishing client-observed goodput from server-internal engine throughput is critical in geo-distributed evaluation. Standard single-node deployments frequently report vLLM's internal Prometheus metrics (such as \texttt{avg\_}\allowbreak\texttt{generation\_}\allowbreak\texttt{throughput\_}\allowbreak\texttt{tok\_s} logged on \texttt{127.0.0.1}). In a distributed multi-tier architecture, relying on localhost engine metrics introduces three fundamental flaws:
(1)~\emph{Network Transport Blindness:} Engine counters track only GPU CUDA forward passes on localhost, remaining blind to OS socket buffers (\texttt{SO\_SNDBUF}, \texttt{tcp\_wmem}), WireGuard cryptographic encapsulation, MTU fragmentation, WAN congestion pacing, and HTTP SSE stream framing at the edge gateway. Internal rates conceal transport stalls behind compute metrics.
(2)~\emph{Continuous Batching Conflation:} Internal engine counters aggregate tokens across continuous batching iterations, conflating prompt prefill processing, KV-cache block allocation, and iteration scheduling with autoregressive decoding.
(3)~\emph{Empirical Discovery of Transport Invariance:} Measuring client-observed goodput via Equation~\eqref{eq:rdecode} established that decode throughput remains invariant at $35.2$--$35.5$~tok/s across all regimes ($0$--$80$~ms RTT, clean to 2\% loss). This proves that wide-area delay bottlenecks solely the first token; once streaming commences, token delivery operates at line rate without bubbles. Localhost logs could never validate this network transparency.

\subsection{Dynamic Overlap Threshold Heuristics}
In SPHP, speculation commitment is governed by the set-overlap verification barrier $\text{Overlap}(\mathcal{H}, \mathcal{F}) \ge \theta$. While static $\theta = 0.5$ delivers robust acceleration ($h = 0.900$), workloads with variable query clarity benefit from dynamic threshold calibration. We formalize an adaptive heuristic using BM25 score dispersion: $\Delta_{\mathrm{BM25}} = s_{\mathrm{BM25}}(d_1) - s_{\mathrm{BM25}}(d_{k_h})$, where $s_{\mathrm{BM25}}(d_i)$ is the lexical score of candidate $i$. Steep dispersion ($\Delta_{\mathrm{BM25}} \gg 0$) indicates high lexical confidence, enabling aggressive speculation ($\theta \to 0.4$); conversely, flat distributions reflect ambiguity, warranting conservative verification ($\theta \to 0.7$) to safeguard cloud GPU bandwidth.

\subsection{Limitations \& Systems Generalizability}
Three systems dimensions contextualize our findings. First, candidate agreement ($h=0.900$) was characterized on MS~MARCO; specialized corpora with divergent vocabulary distributions may exhibit different hit rates, requiring empirical threshold calibration. Second, our benchmarks evaluate sequential streams to isolate latency without multi-tenant GPU queueing; high-concurrency deployments should combine SPHP with prefill admission control and dynamic speculation throttling. Third, dense retrieval was profiled on an edge-class GPU (GTX~1660~Ti); while absolute stage durations scale with hardware compute, the core principles---the dominance of KV-cache residency over network delay and the uniform optimality of SPHP across WAN RTTs---generalize across distributed topologies.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented a comprehensive measurement study of stage-to-node placement for hybrid RAG across edge-cloud topologies. KV-cache state dominates latency variance ($2.8$--$3.2\times$), whereas TTFT is essentially flat in WAN RTT ($\approx$0.95~ms/ms). SPHP eliminates the prefill bottleneck by speculatively overlapping masked prefill with in-flight dense retrieval, cutting cold TTFT by 23.7\%--27.6\% ($p \le 0.0008$, $h=0.900$). Our validated analytical cost model confirms that SPHP split placement is globally optimal. Physical three-node tiering, live public WAN routes, edge failure recoveries, and multi-leg loss stress matrices demonstrate that speculative prefill acceleration provides robust, self-healing performance across distributed environments.
